Theorem 1.1

Potts zero-freeness near \([0,1]\)

Paper

Theorem 1.1 · Main paper
Let \(q,\Deg\) be integers with \(\Deg\ge2\) and \(q\ge11\Deg/6\). There exists \(\eps=\eps(q,\Deg)>0\) such that, for every finite simple graph \(G\) of maximum degree at most \(\Deg\) and every pinning \(\tau\), \[\nZpin{G}{\tau}(z)\ne0 \qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal U_\eps([0,1]).\] Consequently, \(\Zpin{G}{\tau}(z)=z^{m_G(\tau)}\nZpin{G}{\tau}(z)\) has no zeros in the same neighbourhood except, when \(m_G(\tau)\ge1\), a zero of multiplicity \(m_G(\tau)\) at \(z=0\). In particular, \(\Zpart_G\), and \(\Zpin{G}{\tau}\) for every \(\tau\) proper on \(G[\Lambda]\), have no zeros in \(\mathcal U_\eps([0,1])\).

Theorem 1.2

Coupling independence implies zero-freeness

Paper

Theorem 1.2 · Main paper
Fix integers \(q,\Deg\) with \(\Deg\ge2\) and \(q\ge\Deg+1\), and let \(\mathcal G\) be a class of finite simple graphs of maximum degree at most \(\Deg\) that is closed under taking induced subgraphs. Assume:
  1. \(\mathcal G\) satisfies coupling independence at \(x=0\) with constant \(C_0\);
  2. for every \(\delta\in(0,1]\), it satisfies coupling independence on \([\delta,1]\) with a finite constant \(C_\delta\).
Then there is \(\eps>0\) such that, for every \(G\in\mathcal G\) and every pinning \(\tau\), \[\nZpin{G}{\tau}(z)\ne0 \qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal U_\eps([0,1]).\] Consequently, \(\Zpin{G}{\tau}(z)=z^{m_G(\tau)}\nZpin{G}{\tau}(z)\) has no zeros in the same neighbourhood except, when \(m_G(\tau)\ge1\), a zero of multiplicity \(m_G(\tau)\) at \(z=0\).

Definition 2.2

Potts coupling independence

Paper

Definition 2.2 · Main paper
Let \(C\in[0,\infty)\), \(\mathcal G\subseteq\Gdeg\), and \(J\subseteq[0,1]\). We say that \(\mathcal G\) satisfies \(C\)-coupling independence on \(J\) if, for every \(G\in\mathcal G\), every pinning \(\tau\), every \(r\in V^\tau\), all \(a,b\in\colours\), and every \(x\in J\), the two pinned laws are well defined and \[\WHam\!\left( \mupin{G}{\tau^{r=a}}{x}, \mupin{G}{\tau^{r=b}}{x} \right)\le C.\] Both laws live on the common free set \(V^\tau\setminus\{r\}\), where the Hamming distance is taken; at \(x=0\) they are the normalized hard pinned laws. When \(J=\{x\}\), we say that \(\mathcal G\) satisfies \(C\)-coupling independence at \(x\).

Theorem 4.1

Hamming coupling independence on \([0,1]\)

Paper

Theorem 4.1 · Main paper
Let \(q,\Deg\) be positive integers with \(\Deg\ge2\) and \(q>11\Deg/6\). Put \[\gapq:=\frac q\Deg-\frac{11}{6}>0.\] Then, for every \(G\in\Gdeg\), every pinning \(\tau\), every \(x\in[0,1]\), every \(r\in V^\tau\), and all \(a,b\in\colours\), \[\WHam\!\left( \mupin{G}{\tau^{r=a}}{x}, \mupin{G}{\tau^{r=b}}{x} \right) \le \frac{2(1-x)\Deg}{q-(11/6)(1-x)\Deg} \le\frac2{\gapq}.\] At \(x=0\) both laws are well defined by Lemma 2.1, since the hypotheses imply \(q\ge\Deg+2\).
